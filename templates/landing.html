FREQUENTLY ASKED

Questions & answers.

What diseases can RetinaScope detect? ▼
RetinaScope AI classifies retinal fundus images into 5 categories: Normal (healthy retina), Diabetic Retinopathy, Glaucoma, Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD), and Cataract.
What model architecture is used? ▼
We use MobileNetV2 pre-trained on ImageNet with a custom classification head. The model is fine-tuned using a two-phase training strategy — first training the classification head, then fine-tuning the top 30 layers of MobileNetV2.
Does this replace a doctor's diagnosis? ▼
No. RetinaScope AI is a research and educational tool designed to assist screening workflows. It should never be used as a replacement for professional ophthalmological evaluation. Always consult a qualified eye care professional.
What image formats are supported? ▼
The scanner accepts PNG, JPEG, BMP, TIFF, and WebP format retinal fundus photographs up to 16 MB. Images are automatically resized to 224 × 224 for model inference.
How does the preprocessing pipeline work? ▼
Each image passes through a 5-stage pipeline: green channel extraction for vascular contrast, FOV masking to isolate the retinal field, CLAHE for adaptive contrast enhancement, Top-Hat morphological filtering for micro-structure detection, and finally softmax inference through MobileNetV2.
Can I run this offline? ▼
Yes. The entire platform runs locally with Flask, TensorFlow, and OpenCV. No internet connection is required once dependencies are installed. The 19.6 MB Keras model file runs on CPU without any GPU.
